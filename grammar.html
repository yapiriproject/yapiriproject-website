Grammar architecture

From speech sounds to complete discourse.

A strong grammar should explain patterns, show natural examples, identify alternatives, and state where usage varies. Each section should link to dictionary entries and corpus examples.

Sounds and writing

Introduce the sound inventory first, then show how Roman Kokborok and Yapiri represent those sounds. Separate phonemes from letters, and clearly explain tone, aspiration, nasals, stops, glides, and diphthongs.

SchwaRoman w; dedicated Yapiri character; normally medial rather than word-initial.
Aspiratesph, th, kh, ch are taught as distinct digraph units.
High toneDocument the mark, distribution, minimal contrasts, and natural sentence examples.

Word classes and morphology

Give every major part of speech its own chapter. Explain inflection, derivation, compounding, reduplication where relevant to the language, and the difference between productive rules and lexicalised forms.

Phrase structure

Show the internal order of noun phrases, postpositional phrases, verb phrases, numeral expressions, modifiers, possessors, and coordination.

Clause and sentence patterns

Document basic constituent order, negation, questions, commands, comparison, subordination, relative clauses, reported speech, and discourse-level patterns using corpus-linked examples.

Recommended grammar record

Rule name · plain-language explanation · formal description · Roman examples · Yapiri examples · morpheme-by-morpheme gloss · English translation · exceptions · variation note · corpus links · sources.
